# Orange: The Healer's Resolve — Chapter Five: The First Limit (Part 1)

Serra tests Orange immediately. Farming task turns into brutal training. Push him until he fails (important for growth). First time he reaches his healing LIMIT.

Edward introduces Serra to Orange and his parents. They agree on the training and start the day after. Orange is brought to the adventurers guild with his own room, moving out of his parents' house as part of training. Woken very early for running. Not tiring at first, so Serra adds weights, more and more, until he tires and must heal himself. Super tiring and exhausting; he starves too. At the tavern they eat their fill. As a kid he gets no alcohol — carrot juice instead, the Ylila specialty, and the team has the same. Training evolves with more exercises through the week. Running plus field work. End goal of village training: do the whole village's farming single-handedly with ease. Serra encourages him with wisdom throughout. Body beaten by training, character filled with words of wisdom, faith, and devotion. The can-do kid, now exhausted. Mana drained, body freshly healed from broken. Pushed to his limit, a new dawn strings up hope.

---

**Orange: The Healer's Resolve**
**Chapter Five: The First Limit**

Morning came earlier than it ever had before.

---

"Up."

---

Orange opened his eyes.

It was still dark.

---

Serra stood at the doorway of his new room inside the adventurers' hall—arms crossed, expression unreadable.

---

"…It's not morning yet," Orange muttered, still half-asleep.

---

Serra turned.

"Good. Then you're already late."

---

And just like that—

it began.

---

The day before, everything had changed.

---

Edward had brought Serra to his home.

Orange stood beside his parents, unsure, but steady.

---

"This is Serra," Edward said. "She will guide your son."

---

Orange's mother knelt beside him, placing a hand on his shoulder.

"…Do you want this?"

---

Orange thought for a moment.

Then nodded.

"I want to learn."

---

His father exhaled slowly.

"…Then don't quit halfway."

---

"I won't."

---

Serra watched the exchange quietly.

Then smirked.

"…We'll see."

---

By sunset, Orange had moved into the adventurers' hall.

A small room.

A simple bed.

A new life.

---

Now—

his first day had come.

---

"Outside," Serra said.

---

They ran.

---

At first—

it was easy.

---

Orange's steps were light.

His breathing steady.

The familiar warmth already supporting him from within.

---

Serra glanced back.

"…Comfortable?"
---

"…Yes."

---

"Good."

---

She stopped.

Reached into her pack.

And tossed something at him.

---

Orange caught it.

Weights.

---

"Put them on."

---

He did.

---

They ran again.

---

This time—

he felt it.

---

The pull.

The strain.

---

But still—

manageable.

---

"…Still comfortable?" Serra asked.

---

"…A little harder."

---

She smiled.

---

"Not enough."

---

More weights.

---

Then more.

---

Again.

Run.

---

Again.

Run.

---

Again—

---

Orange stumbled.

---

His breath broke.

His legs burned.

His arms dragged like stone.

---

The warmth surged—

reacting—

healing—

restoring—

---

But this time—

it wasn't enough.

---

"…Don't stop," Serra said flatly.

---

"I'm—still—running…" Orange forced out.

---

"Not with your body."

---

Her eyes locked onto him.

---

"With your will."

---

He gritted his teeth.

---

The warmth flared again.

Stronger.

Faster.

---

His body pushed forward.

---

Step—

---

Step—

---

Step—

---

Until—

---

"…Stop."

---

Orange collapsed.

---

Face-first into the dirt.

---

He tried to move.

Couldn't.

---

His chest heaved violently.

His limbs trembled uncontrollably.

---

The warmth flickered.

---

Weak.

---

"…Get up."

---

He didn't.

---

Not because he wouldn't.

---

Because—

he couldn't.

---

Silence.

---

Then—

Serra crouched beside him.

---

"…Good."

---

Orange barely heard it.

---

"…That's your first real step."

---

She stood again.

---

"You finally reached a point where your healing couldn't keep up."

---

A pause.

---

"Remember that feeling."

---

Orange's fingers twitched against the dirt.

---

"…That…"

He struggled to speak.

"…felt like breaking…"

---

Serra nodded.

---

"It was."

---

She turned away.

---

"And you didn't die."

---

---

Later—

they sat at the tavern.

---

Orange looked like he had just come back from war.

---

Exhausted.

Empty.

---

A drink was placed in front of him.

---

"…Carrot juice," Serra said.

---

He blinked.

---

"…That's it?"
---

The swordsman beside them chuckled.

"Kid, that's the strongest thing in this village."

---

Even the archer gave a small nod.

---

Reluctantly—

Orange drank.

---

"…It's good…"

---

Serra smirked.

---

"Of course it is. Now eat."

---

Food followed.

Plates filled.

Then emptied.

Then filled again.

---

Orange ate like he had never eaten before.

---

Because he hadn't.

---

For the first time—

his body wasn't recovering faster than it was breaking.

---

It was catching up.

Barely.

---

Days passed.

---

The training didn't ease.

---

It worsened.

---

Running became routine.

Weights increased.

Distance stretched.

---

Then—

Serra added more.

---

"Fields."

---

"What?"
---

"You're a farmer, right?"
---

"…Yes."

---

"Good."

---

That was when the real training began.

---

Plowing.

Carrying.

Planting.

Watering.

---

But not like before.

---

Everything was heavier.

Faster.

Longer.

---

"Again."

---

"I just finished—"

---

"Again."

---

"…Yes, ma'am."

---

The warmth became his lifeline.

---

Heal.

Move.

Break.

Restore.

---

Again.

---

Again.

---

Again.

---

Until—

---

"…Why…"

Orange gasped, barely holding himself upright.

"…why this kind of training…?"
---

Serra stood beside him, watching the fields.

---

"…Because this is life."

---

She pointed forward.

---

"You don't get stronger in comfort."

---

"You don't learn limits when nothing pushes you."

---

"And you don't understand healing…"

---

"…until you experience breaking."

---

Orange clenched his fists.

---

"…I understand…"

---

Serra shook her head.

---

"No."

---

She stepped closer.

---

"You're starting to."

---

A pause.

---

"Real strength…"

---

Her voice softened—but did not weaken.

---

"…is not how much you can force."

---

"It's how much you can carry."

---

"How much you can endure…"

---

"…without losing who you are."

---

Orange looked at his hands.

Shaking.

Bruised.

Healing.

---

"…Then I'll keep going."

---

Serra smiled faintly.

---

"I know."

---

---

By the end of the week—

Orange stood alone in the field.

---

The tools lay beside him.

The work unfinished.

---

His body—

at its limit.

---

No warmth came.

---

No strength answered.

---

Only silence.

---

"…Move…"

---

Nothing.

---

"…Heal…"

---

Nothing.

---

His vision blurred.

---

"…Please…"

---

Still—

nothing.

---

And for the first time since he discovered his power—

---

Orange felt it.

---

True emptiness.

---

He fell to his knees.

---

"…I can't…"

---

Tears hit the soil beneath him.

---

"…I can't keep going…"

---

Silence answered.

---

Then—

a memory.

---

*"…You gave it."*

---

His prayer.

From that day.

---

His hands trembled.

Then slowly—

he pressed them against the ground.

---

"…I don't have anything left…"

---

His voice cracked.

---

"…but I'll still give what I have…"

---

"…even if it's nothing."

---

The wind moved.

---

Soft.

---

Quiet.

---

And somewhere—

deep within—

---

something responded.

---

Not strength.

---

Not power.

---

But—

peace.

---

Orange closed his eyes.

---

His breathing steadied.

---

His body didn't rise.

---

But his heart did.

---

"…Then I'll start again tomorrow."

---

From a distance—

Serra watched.

---

Silent.

---

Then—

she smiled.

---

"…You passed."

---

Because this—

was the real test.

---

Not strength.

---

Not endurance.

---

But surrender.

---

The boy who could heal endlessly—

---

had finally learned—

---

what it meant

to reach his limit.
