# Orange: The Healer's Resolve — Chapter Three: The Weight of a Gift (Part 1)

The village had never been so loud.

---

By the time the children returned, word had already spread.

"They're back!"
"They're safe!"
"Someone call their parents—!"

---

Amy's mother was the first to arrive.

She dropped everything—basket, cloth, dignity—and ran.

"Amy!"

She pulled her daughter into a tight embrace, hands frantically checking her arms, her legs, her face.

"You're alright… you're alright…"

---

Leo's father wasn't far behind.

"What were you thinking?!" he barked, though his voice trembled.

His grip on Leo's shoulder was firm—too firm.

"You don't cross the border! Not ever!"

"I—I'm sorry…" Leo muttered, head down.

---

Sasha had already started crying.

"I didn't know… we didn't know…"

---

Then—

Orange's parents arrived.

---

His mother's eyes locked onto him first.

"…Orange."

Just his name.

Nothing more.

---

He stepped forward.

"I'm okay."

---

She pulled him in without another word.

Tight.

Stronger than usual.

---

His father stood beside them, arms crossed—but his shoulders lowered ever so slightly.

"…You came back," he said.

---

Relief came first.

Then—

discipline.

---

"You will not leave the field boundaries again," his father continued, voice steady but firm. "Not until you understand what's out there."

Orange nodded.

"I understand."

---

But the village's attention…

did not stay on the danger.

---

It shifted.

Slowly.

Then all at once.

---

"…They said you healed her."

The voice came from behind.

Old Man Ruel, one of the elders, stepped forward, eyes sharp despite his age.

---

All eyes turned to Orange.

---

Amy stepped forward.

"It's true," she said, lifting her leg slightly. "It was worse before… but now it's gone."

---

Murmurs spread.

"A healer?"
"At his age?"
"No incantation?"
"That's not normal…"

---

Orange felt it again.

Not warmth.

Not warning.

---

Pressure.

---

By evening, the entire village had gathered.

At the center stood the chief.

---

Edward.

---

Once, long ago, he had stood on battlefields.

Now, he stood before farmers.

But the weight he carried had not changed.

---

"…We will hear everything," he said calmly.

---

And they did.

---

The story was told.

The running.
The border.
The slime.
The injuries.

And finally—

the healing.

---

Silence followed.

Heavy.

Thinking.

---

Then—

the voices began.

---

"We can't ignore this."
"He's gifted."
"This could change our village."
"Or put it at risk."

---

An officer stepped forward, armor lightly worn but maintained.

"We should consider its value," he said bluntly. "A healer like him—people would pay. Nobles, merchants… even soldiers. This is an opportunity."

---

Murmurs of agreement followed.

---

Another voice cut in.

"No."

It was one of the elder women.

"He's a child. Not a commodity."

---

A farmer raised his hand.

"Then we keep him here. Build something—a clinic. Let him help our own."

---

"And when others hear of it?" someone countered. "You think they won't come? You think they won't take him?"

---

"Then train him," another voice said. "Put him with the militia. If he has power, he should learn to survive."

---

"He belongs in the fields!" an older man argued. "That's his family's life. Don't drag him into things he doesn't need."

---

The voices overlapped.

Clashing.

Pulling Orange in different directions—

as if he were no longer standing among them…

but being divided between them.

---

Edward raised his hand.

Silence followed.

---

"…Enough."

---

His gaze moved to Orange.

Long.

Measured.

---

"…We will not decide this today."

---

Some protested.

He ignored them.

---

"The children are safe. That is what matters."

---

A pause.

Then—

"Tonight, we give thanks."

---

And just like that—

the tension broke.

---

Food was brought out.

Fires were lit.

Laughter returned—though quieter now, edged with something uncertain.

---

But even as the village celebrated—

decisions were already moving.

---

At dawn, a small unit was dispatched.

To the border.

To confirm the children's story.

To ensure the slimes had not moved closer.

---

Precaution.

---

Responsibility.

---

And in the chief's home—

a different decision was being made.

---

Edward sat across from Orange's parents.

A single lantern lit the room.

---

"…Your son is not ordinary," he said plainly.

---

They did not argue.

---

"We know," Orange's mother replied quietly.

---

Edward leaned back slightly.

"I was once an adventurer."

---

That, they knew.

But he rarely spoke of it.

---

"There are things in this world… that cannot be handled by instinct alone."

His gaze sharpened.

"He needs guidance."

---

Orange's father frowned.

"…From who?"

---

Edward exhaled once.

Then—

a faint smile.

---

"…An old friend."

---

"Her name is Serra."

---

Even saying it carried weight.

---

"They called her the *Wild Healer*."

---

Orange's mother blinked. "…A healer?"

---

Edward chuckled softly.

"Yes."

Then after a pause—

"…and no."

---

"She didn't stay behind the lines like most."

---

He leaned forward slightly.

---

"She stood in the middle of battle."

---

"Between front and back."

---

"Healed. Strengthened. Commanded."

---

"And when needed—"

---

"…she fought."

---

The room fell silent.

---

"She led her own party," Edward continued. "Rescue missions. Escorts. High-risk paths most would avoid."

---

"Her strength wasn't just her healing."

---

"It was her judgment."

---

"Her awareness."

---

"…and her refusal to let others fall if she could still stand."

---

Orange's father crossed his arms.

"…And you think she can teach him?"

---

Edward shook his head.

"…No."

---

A pause.

---

"I think she can *shape* him."

---

Silence settled again.

---

"…Will she come?" Orange's mother asked.

---

Edward stood.

"I'll send a letter."

---

He moved to his desk.

Ink.

Paper.

Purpose.

---

"Asking her is easy."

---

He began to write.

---

"…Convincing her…"

---

A small smile formed.

---

"…that's the real challenge."

---

The ink flowed steadily.

Each word carrying weight beyond the page.

---

*Come.*

*There is a boy here.*

*A healer.*

*But more than that—*

*someone who may one day have everything.*

---

He paused.

Then added—

---

*Help him become someone who knows what to do with it.*

---

The letter was sealed.

---

And far beyond the quiet village…

a path had already begun to form.

---

One that would not be walked in comfort.

---

Because the one they had called—

did not believe in easy training.

---

To Serra—

labor was learning.

---

To understand life—

you must work it.

Break it.

Restore it.

---

"If you can work and not get tired…"

---

"Then you have not trained enough."

---

"Do more."

---

"Find your limit."

---

"And then…"

---

"Go beyond it."

---

The dawn of healing had come.

---

Now—

its weight would follow.
