# Orange: The Healer's Resolve — Chapter One: The Boy Who Didn't Break

The sun had not yet risen, but Orange was already awake.

The air was cold, clinging to his skin like a warning, yet he stood barefoot in the fields, gripping a worn wooden hoe nearly as tall as he was. The soil beneath him was dark and heavy from last night's rain, stubborn and unyielding.

Just the way he liked it.

He drove the blade into the earth.

Once.
Twice.
Again.

Each strike sent a dull shock through his arms, the kind that made muscles scream and joints protest. For most children his age, it would have been unbearable.

For Orange—

it was routine.

---

"Don't push yourself too hard."

His mother's voice drifted from behind him, soft but firm.

She stood at the edge of the field, a basket in her arms, worry written plainly across her face. She had said those same words every morning for as long as he could remember.

Orange paused, glancing back at her.

"I'm not," he replied simply.

It wasn't a lie.

---

Because even as the strain built in his body, something else moved quietly beneath it.

A warmth.

Faint at first—like embers buried under ash—but always there.

When his muscles tightened, it loosened them.
When his breath grew heavy, it steadied it.
When fatigue crept in, it erased it.

Not instantly.

Not perfectly.

But enough.

Always enough.

---

He turned back to the field.

*Again,* he told himself.

The hoe rose and fell.

This time, he focused.

Not on the soil.

Not on the motion.

But on that feeling.

---

It had taken him years to notice it.

Longer still to understand that it wasn't normal.

Other children rested when they were tired.
They cried when they were hurt.

Orange… didn't.

Or rather—

he didn't stay that way for long.

---

He drove the blade deeper than before.

The impact rattled through his arms—

and something *strained*.

There.

He caught it.

That subtle resistance beneath the warmth. That invisible thread pulling everything back into place.

---

*What are you?*

---

He tightened his grip and struck again, harder.

Pain flared across his palms.

Skin split.

A thin line of red surfaced.

Orange froze.

---

Slowly, he lifted his hand.

The cut was shallow, but fresh. Real.

For a moment, it simply… existed.

Then—

it faded.

Not vanished instantly.

But *closed*.

The blood slowed.
The skin pulled itself together.
The sting dulled into nothing.

---

Orange stared at it, unmoving.

"…I did that."

It wasn't a question.

---

From the edge of the field, his mother called again.

"Orange, breakfast is ready!"

Her voice snapped the moment.

He lowered his hand, clenching it once, testing.

No pain.

No weakness.

Only strength.

---

"I'm coming," he answered.

But his gaze lingered on his palm.

---

That day, he worked harder than ever before.

Not because he was told to.

But because he *wanted to see*.

---

He carried sacks heavier than usual.

He ran farther than needed.

He pushed until his breathing broke rhythm and his legs trembled—

and then watched.

Felt.

Learned.

---

The warmth responded.

Not randomly.

Not vaguely.

But *precisely*.

---

When his legs weakened, it gathered there.

When his arms strained, it followed.

When his breathing faltered, it settled in his chest, steadying each inhale like invisible hands guiding him back into rhythm.

---

It was not just healing.

It was *listening*.

---

By midday, Orange collapsed onto the grass, staring up at the sky.

Clouds drifted lazily above, untouched by effort or expectation.

"…So that's how it works."

He raised his hand again, turning it slowly.

If he focused—

really focused—

he could feel it move.

Like water flowing beneath skin.

Like something alive, but obedient.

---

"Then…"

His voice was quiet.

"…what else can I do?"

---

The question hung in the air, unanswered.

But something deep within him stirred—

not in response to curiosity alone…

but to something greater.

Something that felt—

given.

---

Orange sat up slowly.

For the first time in his life, the field no longer felt like the boundary of his world.

It felt like the beginning of it.

---

Far beyond the rolling lands he had known all his life, there were cities that burned with magic, forests that whispered with ancient voices, and battlefields where warriors clashed against creatures born from nightmare.

A world vast, broken, and waiting.

---

Orange clenched his hand once more.

Not in fear.

Not in doubt.

But in quiet resolve.

---

"I'll figure it out."

---

Not for power.

Not for pride.

But because whatever this was—

this strength, this healing, this endless well within him—

was never meant to be wasted.

---

And somewhere, beyond the fields he had always known—

someone would need it.

---

The boy who never broke…

had finally begun to understand why.

---

And this time—

he would not stop at surviving.

He would learn.

He would grow.

And one day—

he would choose what to do with everything he had been given.
