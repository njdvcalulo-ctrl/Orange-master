# YLILA VILLAGE SYSTEM (ENHANCED – FINAL LOCK)

## Scale & Layout

Population: ~150 people. Size: ~10–15 minutes to cross on foot. Radial layout: village center at the core, homes branching outward, farms on the outer ring, forest + lake as natural borders.

## Key Locations (Finalized)

Village Center: Chief's House & Office (governance + "police"), small open market space, occasional merchant stalls, community gathering point. Chief = only formal authority + strongest fighter (early story).

The Inn (1 only): rest stop for traveling merchants, occasional adventurers passing through, early exposure to outside world rumors.

Mini Adventurer Hub (small guild outpost, not a full branch): quest board + appraisal desk (sometimes manned). Mostly pest control, herb gathering, delivery tasks. Where kids first see the adventurer system.

Oathkeeper Farm: slightly outside main cluster, highly fertile land (subtle hint of Orange's influence later), one of the most productive farms.

Lake Area: clean freshwater source (secondary to well/stream), fishing zone, calm scenic area. Amy's house nearby — parents Steve & Naomi (fisherfolk; blade skills via filleting → combat influence, dance → movement).

Leo's House (near center, close to Orange's home): father Ethan, village blacksmith (tools, repairs, light weapons); mother Nathaly, artisan/designer. Early exposure to durability, defense, equipment importance.

Lyra's House (near forest edge, slightly isolated): father Vincent, carpenter (built her treehouse); mother Silk, forager and tailor. Features: treehouse observation point, natural scouting advantage.

Training Clearing: open space near village edge. Kids spar, run, play-fight. Skill discovery, early system exposure, team dynamics.

Forest Edge: herbs, wood, small animals. Mild danger: low-tier monsters (later introduction).

Storage Barns: shared grain reserves. Symbol of village survival.

## Social Structure (Refined)

Tight-knit community, everyone knows everyone. Skill-based respect system (not class-based yet). Common roles: farmers, fisherfolk, blacksmith, carpenter, tailor, herb gatherers.

## Combat Culture

No standing army. Only Chief = real combat authority. Kids play-fight, imitate adventurers, use wooden weapons. Makes later growth feel earned.

## Village Life System (Scene Generator)

1. Morning Routine: roosters and early wake-up, parents assigning chores, water fetching and tool prep, farmers heading out. Calm, grounded, disciplined.
2. Work Phase: farming, fishing, crafting. Kids helping (or sneaking off). Small accidents — cuts, bruises → Orange moments. Good for subtle power reveals and bonding.
3. Midday Break: shared meals, storytelling, teasing between friends. Builds relationships and warmth.
4. Afternoon / Training: kids go to clearing. Sparring, skill calling, friendly rivalry. Introduces combat system and class tendencies.
5. Evening Wind-Down: families regroup, market interactions, repairs, cooking.
6. Night (VERY IMPORTANT FOR ORANGE): quiet village. Secrets happen here — healing practice, reflection, emotional processing. Orange's hidden growth zone.

## Why This Works

Consistency (scenes always make sense), emotional weight (readers feel the stakes), contrast (peaceful village vs future war), natural progression (play → real combat).

## Final Note

Ylila is not just a setting. It is Orange's baseline, his resting place, his reason to fight. When things go wrong later… this peaceful system is what breaks the reader.
