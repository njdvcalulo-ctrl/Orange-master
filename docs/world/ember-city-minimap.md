# Ember City Mini Map (Locked Layout)

Overview: walled circular city. Organized on the surface, oppressive
underneath. Hidden power structure behind the noble facade. Existing
locks hold: Sebastians are the noble house; Ember keeps its name after
the fall, only leadership changes; food shortage plus civil unrest;
Ember delays invasion through internal division.

## Districts (Locked)

1. Central Castle: seat of the Dark Knight and the King (exact top
authority decided later). Imposing, distant, unreachable. Heavily guarded.
2. Noble District (north): wealthy families, political influence, quietly
aligned with the ruling force. Not mind-controlled — they benefit from
the system.
3. Merchant District (east-center): trade hub, shops, caravans,
negotiations. Rizla the Merchant Druid lives here — controls supply
chains, trade routes, pricing. This is where Ylila gets scammed and the
first tension builds.
4. Mage Quarter (south-center): research, magic users, academies,
restricted access. The Mage General controls knowledge and magical
enforcement.
5. Industrial Zone (south): workshops, forging, weapon production. The
Engineer General builds war tools and invasion capability.
6. Slums / Outer Ring: dense, poor, ignored — where most citizens
actually live. Carries the food shortage and the unrest.

## Rings (Locked)

```
     |-------------------|
     | INDUSTRIAL ZONE   |  <- weapons / production
     |-------------------|
     | MAGE QUARTER      |  <- magic control
     |-------------------|
     | MERCHANT DISTRICT |  <- trade / Rizla
     |-------------------|
     | NOBLE DISTRICT    |  <- influence
     |-------------------|
     |     CASTLE        |  <- power center
     +-------------------+
```

The deeper you go: richer, cleaner, more controlled — and more
dangerous in a different way.

## Gates & Entry Flow (Locked)

South Gate (main entry, from Ylila): busiest gate — merchants,
travelers, farmers. Guards check goods lightly and are corruptible.
The party always enters here first.
East Gate: lake access — fish trade, water transport, calmer traffic.
North Gate: restricted, leads to deeper lands, rarely used.
West Gate: danger side — leads to the plains and the golems, almost
unused, heavily reinforced and avoided.

## District Feel (Locked — use when writing)

Outer Slums: crowded, loud, smells of food, sweat, and waste. Real
people struggling. Alive but heavy.
Industrial Zone: metal sounds, heat, smoke, workers everywhere.
Pressure, labor, war preparation.
Mage Quarter: quiet, controlled, unnatural calm, guarded. Unnerving.
Merchant District (Rizla zone): clean, organized, busy but controlled,
polished storefronts. Friendly… but fake.
Noble District: quiet, spacious, guarded, untouched by struggle.
Detached power.
Castle: distant, imposing, rarely accessed. Authority.

## Power structure (Locked)

Top: Dark Knight / King (exact identity later). Below: three generals,
independent and aligned by interest, never dominated — intelligent evil,
not mindless. Rizla Viremont holds the economy. Valen Kyr holds magic.
Darius Volkr holds weapons and tech. Full builds in
docs/characters/ember/.

## Contrast with Ylila (Locked)

Ylila is open, natural, warm, honest. Ember is layered, structured,
controlled, calculated. The contrast carries the story emotionally. Movement runs market → noble → castle; arcs split
economic (Rizla), magical, and war buildup.

## Power Flow (Locked — invisible but felt)

```

   v
Nobles (Influence)
   v
Rizla (Economy)
   v
Mage (Control)
   v
Engineer (Production)
   v

```

Pressure runs downhill. The slums carry the weight of every layer
above them — which is why the unrest lives there, not in the castle.
Power flows downward. Pressure builds upward.

## How to Use While Writing (Locked)

Ask every Ember scene: where are they (gate, slums, market)? What do
they feel (heat, pressure, silence)? Who controls this zone (Rizla,
Valen, Darius)?
First visit flow: enter South Gate → notice the crowd → pass the
slums → hear industry → reach the Merchant District → meet Rizla →
get fairly scammed.
Small tells: people don't look long, conversations run short, smiles
are rare.

## City Guard System (Locked)

Core identity: disciplined on the surface, selective in enforcement,
quietly corrupt in layers. Not chaotic evil, not incompetent —
controlled instruments of the city's power.

```
Commander (Castle-appointed)
   v
Captains (District leaders)
   v
Lieutenants (Squad leaders)
   v
City Guards (standard units)
```

The leash sits at the Castle, but enforcement bends district by
district — which is why the South Gate checks goods lightly and the
slums barely see patrols at all.

Commander: answers directly to the Castle, enforces city-wide
directives, rarely seen. Tone: authority.
Captains (one per district, and they define each zone's personality):
Slums — harsh, impatient. Merchant — polite, corrupt. Mage Quarter —
strict, loyal. Industrial — practical.
Standard guards: light armor, short blade or spear, basic shield. Some
know stamina skills and basic formations. Not elite, but coordinated.

Enforcement rule: laws apply based on location plus the value of the
target. Slums — harsh and fast, often unfair: theft means immediate
punishment, no investigation. Merchant District — selective and
negotiable: disputes resolve with coin, guards take bribes. Mage
Quarter — strict and absolute: illegal magic means instant arrest.
Noble District — minimal and political: nobles are rarely punished.

Corruption is layered and systemic, never chaotic: common guards take
small bribes to look away or speed things along — not greedy, just used
to it. Lieutenants and captains choose which cases live — reports go
missing, investigations redirect. The top cannot be bribed; its
decisions are strategic. The system benefits the structure and maintains
control.

Behavior: watch quietly, step in only when needed, speak briefly and
direct — "Move along." "State your business." "This is not your
concern." "You'll settle that here."

Special units (future use): Enforcers for escalation; the Mage Watch,
anti-magic guards under Valen; Industrial Guards protecting weapon
production, loyal to Darius.

Story effects: entering Ember, guards don't stop the party harshly but
observe everything. At the Rizla encounter they stand nearby, never
interfering — that silence is power. Later they become obstacle,
pressure, and moral tension. Contrast with Ylila: trust-based vs
rule-based, personal vs systemic, protective vs controlling, known faces
vs uniform authority.

Small tell, big tool: guards remember faces — not at once, but over
time. Suspicion arcs and tracking live here.
